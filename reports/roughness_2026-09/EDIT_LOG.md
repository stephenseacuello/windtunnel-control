# Edit log: report revision of 5 October 2026

Stephen's writing rules ask for targeted edits in this format. This log covers the substantive
edits on `report/report.tex`: the second pass (rules v2) and the third pass (master framework, v3).
Edits that only shorten a sentence without changing its meaning are not listed. The numbers are
LaTeX macros, generated by `src/build_report.py`.

## Second pass (rules v2): targeted edits

**1. Abstract background**
- Original: five sentences of background (fuzzy-skin definition, CAD geometry, Wilcox, Achenbach,
  gap).
- Problem: background outweighed the results (§19 asks for only as much context as the problem
  needs).
- Recommended: three sentences:
  - fuzzy skin varies texture without changing geometry [1];
  - roughness degrades airfoil sections [2] and lowers a cylinder's critical Reynolds number [3];
  - neither predicts the effect on these curved-plate blades.
- Reason: the abstract now prioritises results.

**2. Open-circuit voltage provenance (abstract, conclusion 3)**
- Original: "They came with a higher generator open-circuit voltage at unchanged source
  resistance."
- Problem: V_oc is extrapolated from the V–I fit, not measured, and R_int is an apparent
  resistance (§2, provenance).
- Recommended: "…a higher extrapolated open-circuit voltage at unchanged apparent source
  resistance…"
- Reason: separates calculated quantities from measured ones.

**3. Purpose of the speed measurement (Introduction)**
- Original: "Rotor speed locates the source of a power difference between the rotor and the
  generator."
- Problem: ambiguous. It can be read as a difference between the rotor and the generator.
- Recommended: "Light-load rotor speed shows whether a power difference arises in the rotor or in
  the generator."
- Reason: clarity (§24 item 5).

**4. Labelling sentence (Introduction)**
- Original: "The literature describes texture effects for two other classes of body."
- Problem: an announcing label (§5).
- Recommended: deleted. The paragraph opens with the airfoil finding.
- Reason: concision.

**5. Definition of "rotor" (§2.2)**
- Original: "rotor" and "set" were used interchangeably and never defined.
- Problem: terminology must be defined before use (§17).
- Recommended: "A set mounted on the rig is called a rotor and named after the set."
- Reason: one term per concept.

**6. Missing generator information (§3.2)**
- Original: no statement about the generator.
- Problem: the generator's make, model and winding resistance are not recorded. They bear on how
  R_int is read (§13: flag, don't assume).
- Recommended: "The generator's make, model and winding resistance were not recorded."
  Recommendation 2 now asks for the winding resistance to be measured.
- Reason: reproducibility. The missing information is now explicit.

**7. Light-load speed: assumption and term (§3.3, §5, §7)**

**8. V₁–n₀ relation (§3.3)**

**9. Why the Thévenin fit is used (§5)**

**10. Why C_P,el is used (§5)**

## Third pass (master framework v3): targeted edits

The third pass adjudicated the second audit's findings, in the framework's priority order:
correctness, scope, claim–evidence, provenance, objective alignment, logic, then language.

**15. Cut-out ladders (§4.3, README, data dictionary)**
- Original: "The other two stopped on the load's voltage-floor cut-out after the peak, with the last
  dwell already below 80% of the maximum: … (49%) … (57%) (last dwell relative to the maximum)."
- Problem: incorrect on two counts.
  - The load did not cut out. The host stopped the ladder after two dwells at or below its 0.5 V
    floor, while the current still followed the demand.
  - 49% and 57% are the last dwells above the floor. The last dwells were 6% and 25%.
- Recommended: "…stopped after the peak on the host's voltage floor: the terminal voltage fell below
  0.5 V on two consecutive dwells while the current still followed the demand. In these two
  ladders, …, the last dwell above the floor had already fallen below 80% of the maximum…" (new
  macro `vOff`).
- Reason: correctness. The dwell records and `peak_finder.py` show what happened.

**17. Abstract**

  geometry [1]." There was no statement of why the question matters. "Resolved" was undefined. The
  speed gains carried no averaging range, and the abstract ended on a restatement.
- Problem:
  - "geometry" is wrong: fuzzy skin changes the printed surface, not the CAD model;
  - the citation covered our inference;
  - the problem, the gap and the implication were incomplete (abstract elements);
  - "unchanged" overstated two unresolved differences.
- Recommended:
  - the cited definition is separated from the inference ("…it changes a printed blade's surface
    texture without changing its CAD model");
  - one sentence on why it matters (print settings must be controlled when rotors are compared);
  - "not resolved, its interval including zero";
  - wind speeds "by the tunnel calibration";
  - speed gains "averaged over 12.4–38.0 m/s";
  - "no resolved change/difference";
  - the rotor-side inference stated as an inference;
  - Plain's coarser layer period added to the attribution sentence;
  - a closing implication for the fault tests.
- Reason: correctness, provenance and the abstract's elements. No claim is stronger than in the

## Third pass: recurring weaknesses

**A. Reusable principle (added to the rules).**
- An unresolved difference is not an equality. Write "no resolved difference (largest x [lo, hi])"
  and bound any equality the argument needs by the interval.
- This occurred in six places.

**B. Covered by existing rules.**
- Statistics of different kinds in one clause (a mean range with a single-run maximum) falls under
  numerical fidelity.
- Results stated in Methods (τ, V₁/n₀ between rotors) falls under section discipline.
- A log label taken as a description (`load-cutout`) falls under provenance.
- An either/or that omits alternatives falls under argument and causal discipline.
