% ---------------------------------------------------------------- summary --
\section*{Summary}

\begin{keybox}
\textbf{Bottom line.} Tested on one day against the un-textured rotor, the rotors printed with
\SI{0.101}{mm} and \SI{0.202}{mm} of fuzzy-skin texture produced $\dlvlB\%$ and $\dlvlC\%$
more peak electrical power over \vlo–\vhi\,\si{m/s}. Allowing for mounting variation,
estimated from the earlier runs, the 95\% intervals are $[\dlomB,\ \dhimB]\%$ and
$[\dlomC,\ \dhimC]\%$: both gains are resolved. The \SI{0.050}{mm} rotor's $\dlvlA\%$ is not. The gain sits in the open-circuit voltage,
consistent with the rotor turning faster in the same wind; rotor speed was not measured. In the point estimates power
rises with fuzzy-skin thickness, not with measured roughness: Ra is \sRaB–\sRaC\,\si{\micro m} on
the three textured rotors and \sRaS\,\si{\micro m} on the un-textured one.
\end{keybox}

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2em]
  \item \textbf{Steps.} Of the three steps in thickness, only \SI{0.050}{} to \SI{0.101}{mm} is
        resolved (its 95\% interval excludes zero): $\dstepAB\%$ $[\dsteplomAB,\ \dstephimAB]$
        (\secref{sec:results}). \Ra{40} and \Ra{80} are above the un-textured rotor at all
        \dnsp{} wind speeds.
  \item \textbf{Mechanism.} Open-circuit voltage, a proxy for free-running rotor speed, rises by
        $\dvocB\%$ and $\dvocC\%$ for \Ra{40} and \Ra{80}, while source resistance shows no
        detectable change. Squared, the voltage change implies $\dvocsqB\%$ and $\dvocsqC\%$ in power.
  \item \textbf{Reproducibility.} Each textured rotor's 1 October level is $\xdA\%$, $\xdB\%$
        and $\xdC\%$ above its single run of 20 August–1 September, made in a different order.
  \item \textbf{Caveats.} Each rotor was mounted once on 1 October, so mounting variation comes
        from the earlier runs. The rotors were run in order of increasing texture
        (\secref{sec:drift} gives the evidence against drift). The un-textured rotor was printed
        with \SI{0.2}{mm} layers and the \Ra{20} and \Ra{40} sets with about \SI{0.1}{mm}, so
        the reference differs in more than texture. There is one print per texture level. The rig measures electrical
        power, not the aerodynamic power coefficient.
\end{itemize}

\section{Test articles}\label{sec:specimens}

All rotors share one geometry (\texttt{v1}): three straight, untwisted blades at radius
$R=\SI{0.1016}{m}$ with span $H=\SI{0.2451}{m}$, so the swept area is $2RH=\SI{0.0498}{m^2}$. The
blade section is a thin cambered plate, not an airfoil: a \SI{1.79}{mm} wall on a \SI{48}{mm}
chord with \SI{183}{\degree} of turning and square edges, closer to a half-cylinder bucket than to
a wing. The chord Reynolds number spans $\relo\times10^4$ to $\rehi\times10^5$.

The sets differ in the slicer's fuzzy-skin thickness, which randomly displaces the outer wall and
doubles from one textured set to the next (\tabref{tab:specimens}). The labels \Ra{20}, \Ra{40}
and \Ra{80} are names, not measurements. They come from roughness targets suggested when the
prints were planned (4 August), extended by doubling. Fuzzy-skin thickness is the controlled
variable and is used throughout.

\textbf{Slicer file.} The slicer project \texttt{turbine\_default.3mf} (Bambu Studio, saved
\slsaved) holds \slplates{} plates, each with one blade ($\slsize$\,mm, the \texttt{v1} blade; a
rotor takes three). Two are reported sets: \SI{0.101}{} and \SI{0.202}{mm} (plates ``40 RA'' and
``80 RA''); the third holds a set not reported here. The profile is a \slprinter{} with a
\SI{\slnozzle}{mm} nozzle, \SI{\sllayer}{mm} layers and \slmaterial. Fuzzy skin is painted on both
faces of each blade (\slpaint\% of the surface area; the square edges and end caps are plain) at a
\SI{\slpoint}{mm} point distance. The \SI{\sllayer}{mm} layers match the measured period of
\Ra{40}. The file holds no \SI{0.050}{mm} or un-textured blade. Its material (\slmaterial)
conflicts with the run notes (PETG), and its nozzle with the printer's account (\SI{0.4}{mm}); it
does not record which prints were made from it.

\subsection{Surface measurement}\label{sec:surface}
On 1 October each set was scanned with a Keyence VR-6000: one \sfieldx\,×\,\sfieldy\,mm field
per set (two for \Ra{80}) at \SI{\sdx}{\micro m} per pixel (\figref{fig:surface}). Profiles run
spanwise, across the layer lines, and are levelled. Pa is the arithmetic mean deviation of the
levelled profile; Ra is the same after an ISO 16610-21 Gaussian filter with
$\lambda_c=\SI{0.25}{mm}$ (\appref{app:methods}).
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{The un-textured surface is not smooth.} Its layer lines form a regular
        \SI{\spitchS}{\micro m} ridge pattern, which gives it the highest Ra of the four
        (\SI{\sRaS}{\micro m}).
  \item \textbf{\Ra{20} and \Ra{40} are nearly indistinguishable}: Pa \sPaA{} and
        \SI{\sPaB}{\micro m}, Ra \sRaA{} and \SI{\sRaB}{\micro m}, on the same layer period.
  \item \textbf{\Ra{80} has about twice the Pa} (\SI{\sPaC}{\micro m}). Its two fields differ by
        \SI{\sPaspreadC}{\micro m} (\sPaspreadpctC\%), so one field is a coarse sample.
\end{itemize}

% ============================================================= 2 METHOD ==
\section{Method}\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Rig and protocol}
A Python host commands the tunnel fan through a Portenta Machine Control and an ABB ACS550 drive
(\figref{fig:rig}). The generator feeds a three-phase bridge rectifier and a Chroma 63004 DC
electronic load, which sets the current and measures terminal voltage and current. Wind speed is
the tunnel calibration $v = 0.02132\,N_\text{fan} - 0.424$ (\si{m/s}, rpm; $R^2=0.9996$) applied
to the commanded fan speed.

Each sweep visits \dnsp{} fan set points from \dsplo{} to \dsphi{} rpm (\vlo–\vhi\,\si{m/s}). At
each, the load raises its constant-current demand in \SI{1}{s} dwells (\SI{20}{mA} at 1800 rpm,
scaled with $v^2$ below) and stops when power falls below 80\% of its running maximum. A sweep
takes about \dminutes{} minutes. Every run carries protocol fingerprint \texttt{94bed28333f7}.

  \centering

  \caption{Measurement and control chain. Rotor speed was not recorded.}

\subsection{The 1 October session}
Each rotor was mounted once and swept twice without being removed, in the order no texture,
\Ra{20}, \Ra{40}, \Ra{80}, from \dtfirst{} to \dtlast{} (first to last load step). The two
no-texture runs started \dsmoothgap{} minutes apart; the other pairs ran back to back. \dclean{} of \dtotal{} set points ended on the roll-off rule. The other two ended on the
load's voltage-floor cut-out after power had already fallen well below its peak: \dcutlist{} of
peak. In every ladder power fell after the peak, so every $\pmax$ lies inside the ladder. Air temperature and pressure were not
recorded.

\subsection{Analysis}\label{sec:analysis}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Peak power} is the largest \SI{1}{s} dwell. A parabolic fit through the peak gives
        nearly identical changes (\appref{app:methods}).
  \item \textbf{Comparison.} For each run, $y$ is the mean over the \dnsp{} set points of
        $\ln(\pmax/P_\text{ref})$, where $P_\text{ref}$ is the geometric mean of the two
        no-texture runs at that set point. The run-to-run standard deviation, pooled within
        rotors, is \dsigma\% (\ddof{} degrees of freedom). No rotor was remounted, so this is
        repeatability only.
  \item \textbf{Mounting variation} is estimated from the August–September runs, each a separate
        mounting: their differences from the 1 October means imply a mounting standard deviation
        $\sigma_m=\dsigmam\%$ (\ddofm{} degrees of freedom), which also absorbs any day-to-day
        change. It is assumed the same for the no-texture rotor, which has no earlier run. The headline
        95\% intervals include it (\appref{app:methods}). Print-to-print variation is not
        included.
  \item \textbf{Thévenin decomposition.} At each set point the ladder follows
        $V=V_\mathrm{oc}-IR_\mathrm{int}$ ($r^2\ge\thevminrtwo$ in every run). $V_\mathrm{oc}$
        rises with free-running rotor speed, offset by the rectifier drop; $R_\mathrm{int}$ combines the electrical path with the
        rotor's slowing under load. At the peak, $\pmax\approx V_\mathrm{oc}^2/4R_\mathrm{int}$.
\end{itemize}

% ============================================================= 3 RESULTS ==
\section{Results}\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Peak power}
Every run of every textured rotor lies above both no-texture runs, and the two runs of any rotor
differ by less than 1\% (\figref{fig:day}, \tabref{tab:data}). The August–September runs, each on another mounting
and day, differ from the 1 October means by \dxdlo–\dxdhi\%. Allowing for that mounting variation, the
changes between neighbouring thicknesses are:
\begin{itemize}
  \item no texture to \SI{0.050}{mm}: $\dstepSA\%$ $[\dsteplomSA,\ \dstephimSA]$;
  \item \SI{0.050}{} to \SI{0.101}{mm}: $\dstepAB\%$ $[\dsteplomAB,\ \dstephimAB]$;
  \item \SI{0.101}{} to \SI{0.202}{mm}: $\dstepBC\%$ $[\dsteplomBC,\ \dstephimBC]$.
\end{itemize}
Only the middle step is resolved, meaning its 95\% interval excludes zero. \Ra{80} was above
\Ra{40} in both campaigns ($\lvlBCraw\%$ in August–September, $\dstepBC\%$ on 1 October), but the
1 October interval includes zero. The
\Ra{20}-to-\Ra{40} step is the cleanest texture comparison in the data: by the printer's account
the two sets share a print batch, they share a measured layer period, and they differ otherwise only
by the magnet on one \Ra{40} blade.

\subsection{Variation with wind speed}
\Ra{40} and \Ra{80} are above the un-textured rotor at all \dnsp{} set points, and \Ra{20} at
\dposA{} (\figref{fig:curves}). The gain is not uniform. All three textured rotors gain most at
\dpkvC–\dpkvB\,\si{m/s} (\Ra{80} $\dpkC\%$, \Ra{40} $\dpkB\%$) and least near
\dlovC–\dlovB\,\si{m/s}. Because all three rise together, part of that peak may be a dip in the
reference curve rather than a texture effect; these data do not separate the two.

\subsection{Where the extra power comes from}\label{sec:thevenin}
Relative to the un-textured rotor (\figref{fig:thevenin}; mean over set points, $\pm$ 95\%
half-width across set points):
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Open-circuit voltage} rises by $\dvocA\pm\dvochalfA\%$, $\dvocB\pm\dvochalfB\%$
        and $\dvocC\pm\dvochalfC\%$ for \Ra{20}, \Ra{40} and \Ra{80}. It is higher at
        \dvochigherA, \dvochigherB{} and \dvochigherC{} of \dnsp{} set points.
  \item \textbf{Source resistance} changes by $\drintA\pm\drinthalfA\%$, $\drintB\pm\drinthalfB\%$
        and $\drintC\pm\drinthalfC\%$: no detectable change.
\end{itemize}
The gain therefore sits in $V_\mathrm{oc}$: with $R_\mathrm{int}$ unchanged, its square gives
$\dvocsqA\%$, $\dvocsqB\%$ and $\dvocsqC\%$ against $\dlvlA\%$, $\dlvlB\%$ and $\dlvlC\%$
measured. This decomposes the same ladders; it is not an independent measurement. A higher
$V_\mathrm{oc}$ in the same wind means faster free-running rotation, from more aerodynamic torque,
less parasitic torque, or both. Rotor speed was not measured.

\subsection{Run order and drift}\label{sec:drift}
The rotors were run in order of increasing texture, so a steady drift through the afternoon would
mimic a texture effect. Three observations argue against a large drift; none excludes one of a few
percent.
\begin{enumerate}
  \item The two no-texture runs, \dsmoothgap{} minutes apart, differ by $\dsmoothdiff\%$, within
        run-to-run scatter. Extrapolated over the \dspan-minute session that is about \ddrift\%, but
        one pair cannot exclude more.
  \item The fan drew the same current at every set point in all eight runs, within one
        \SI{0.1}{A} count of the drive's reading (\SI{\dmotorhi}{A} at 1800 rpm). Fan load
        depends on air density, so a large density change is unlikely. This is not a calibrated
        density measurement.
  \item The textured rotors are $\xdA\%$, $\xdB\%$ and $\xdC\%$ above their August–September
        single runs. Those runs were made over 12 days in the order \Ra{20}, \Ra{80},
        \Ra{40}, and put \Ra{40} and \Ra{80} $\lvlABraw\%$ and $\lvlACraw\%$ above \Ra{20},
        against $\dstepAB\%$ and $\dstepAC\%$ on 1 October.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Relation to the Jeong-lab tests}\label{sec:jeong}

Two earlier tests were recorded on the Jeong-lab DAQ: the initial reference (5 June, probably the
original rotor) and a no-texture print (27 July). \figref{fig:jeong} compares both with the rig's
un-textured rotor at matched fan set point.
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{5 June.} At 800–1700 rpm the values differ from the rig's un-textured rotor by
        $\djunlo\%$ to $\djunhi\%$ (geometric mean $\djunmid\%$): inside the band of the
        textured rotors at \djunin{} of \djunn{} set points, above it at \djunabove{} and below
        it at \djunbelow. The rotor differs, and the June values are maxima of short averages,
        which read high against the rig's \SI{1}{s} dwells. This shows only that the printed
        rotors perform roughly like the initial reference.
  \item \textbf{27 July.} The lab's table reports maxima of raw \SI{\jlfs}{Hz} samples, which
        collect current-sensor noise (about $\SI{0.11}{A}\times V$). At 500–700 rpm there was no
        load current (\appref{app:jeong}). Reprocessed on the rig's basis, the July values differ
        from the rig's un-textured rotor by $\djullo\%$ to $\djulhi\%$, below it at all \djuln{}
        usable set points. The gap cannot be read as a rotor difference. The load was held
        below the power peak, so the July values are lower bounds, and the current scale cannot
        be checked from the file. The free-running voltages, which need no current calibration,
        differ by at most \dvfreemax\%: \dvlabFiveZeroZero, \dvlabSixZeroZero{} and
        \SI{\dvlabSevenZeroZero}{V} in the lab against \dvrigFiveZeroZero, \dvrigSixZeroZero{}
        and \SI{\dvrigSevenZeroZero}{V} on the rig at 500, 600 and 700 rpm.
\end{itemize}
Recording the lab DAQ in parallel with the Chroma load during one rig sweep would calibrate the
current channel and give the rig the rotor speed it lacks.

\section{Discussion and limitations}\label{sec:discussion}

\textbf{What is established.} The rotors with \SI{0.101}{} and \SI{0.202}{mm} of fuzzy skin
produce more peak electrical power than the un-textured rotor, by margins that mounting variation
and day-to-day change do not explain. The gain sits in the open-circuit voltage, consistent with
faster free-running rotation. Whether \SI{0.050}{mm} helps, and whether \SI{0.202}{mm} beats
\SI{0.101}{mm}, is not resolved.

\textbf{Which surface property matters.} Ra at $\lambda_c=\SI{0.25}{mm}$ does not rank the rotors
by power: the un-textured rotor has the highest Ra and the lowest power, and \Ra{40} the lowest Ra
and the second-highest power. Pa separates only \Ra{80}. The \Ra{80} map shows features up to
about \SI{1}{mm} across (\figref{fig:surface}), comparable to the \sfieldy\,mm field, so a
\SI{0.25}{mm} cut-off removes much of the texture. Longer profiles are needed before any roughness parameter can be tied to performance.

\textbf{Mechanism.} The free-running tip-speed ratio is about \lamfreelo–\lamfreehi{} (estimated
from $V_\mathrm{oc}$ through the lab's generator constant), and the electrical power coefficient
at peak, $\pmax/(\tfrac12\rho Av^3)$, is \cpelmin–\cpelmax\%. The rotor is drag-driven: its
torque is the difference between drag on the concave advancing face and the convex returning
face. Roughness on the convex face can delay separation and reduce its drag, as on a golf ball.
That is a candidate, not a demonstrated mechanism. Lower parasitic torque would also raise
$V_\mathrm{oc}$, and only a $C_p(\lambda)$ comparison with measured rotor speed can separate the
two. The Sandia roughness study circulated in August \cite{wilcox} concerns airfoils at
utility-scale Reynolds numbers, where roughness is detrimental; it does not apply to this rotor.

% =========================================================== 6 NEXT ==
\section{Next steps}\label{sec:next}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Reprint the no-texture set} (one blade broke) at \SI{0.1}{mm} layers, as for
        \Ra{20} and \Ra{40}, and confirm every set's settings from its slicer (\texttt{.3mf}) file.
  \item \textbf{Repeat with remounting, in an interleaved order}: remove and remount each rotor
        between runs, and re-run the reference every few runs.
  \item \textbf{Extend the surface measurement}: several fields per set, longer profiles, and the
        convex face near the leading edge.
  \item \textbf{Add rotor speed and ambient air} to every run, and record one sweep with the lab
        DAQ in parallel.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Toward the fault-testing plan}\label{sec:faults}
Prof.\ Jeong asked for a fault-testing plan agreed before the next round. The campaign suggests a
starting point.
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{What the rig offers.} An automated sweep takes \dminutes{} minutes, with a
        run-to-run repeatability of \dsigma\% and a mounting-plus-day variation estimated at \dsigmam\%
        from three comparisons. The Thévenin decomposition helps separate
        rotor-side faults, which move $V_\mathrm{oc}$, from electrical ones, which move
        $R_\mathrm{int}$; rotor-side drag can move $R_\mathrm{int}$ too. The tunnel node's accelerometer adds vibration spectra.
  \item \textbf{Candidate faults.} A tip mass on one blade (imbalance); a chipped, shortened or
        missing blade; added bearing drag; an open or high-resistance generator phase.
  \item \textbf{Proposed next step.} A short meeting to agree the fault list, severity levels and
        acceptance criteria before any fault blades are printed.
\end{itemize}

\section{Methods detail}\label{app:methods}
\textbf{Peak power.} The dwells in which the load tracked its demand with current above zero form
the ladder $(I_k,P_k)$. The raw estimate is $\max_k P_k$. The fit takes the arg\,max and up to two
dwells either side, fits $P=a_2I^2+a_1I+a_0$, and reports the vertex if $a_2<0$ and it lies inside
the window; otherwise the arg\,max. This is a port of the rig software's rule. On the fit basis the
changes are $\dlvlfitA\%$ $[\dlofitA,\ \dhifitA]$, $\dlvlfitB\%$ $[\dlofitB,\ \dhifitB]$ and
$\dlvlfitC\%$ $[\dlofitC,\ \dhifitC]$ (run-to-run intervals, $\sigma=\dsigmafit\%$), against
$\dlvlA\%$, $\dlvlB\%$ and $\dlvlC\%$ on the arg\,max.

\textbf{Run comparison.} Run $j$ of rotor $r$ gives
$y_{rj}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_s\ln(P_{js}/P_{\text{ref},s})$ over the $n=\dnsp$ set points common to every
run. The pooled standard deviation is
$\sigma^2=\sum_r\sum_j(y_{rj}-\bar y_r)^2/\sum_r(n_r-1)$. The change between rotors $a$ and $b$ is
$\exp(\bar y_a-\bar y_b)-1$, with interval
$\exp\!\big(\bar y_a-\bar y_b \pm t_{0.975,\ddof}\,\sigma\sqrt{1/n_a+1/n_b}\big)-1$ for
run-to-run repeatability.

\textbf{Mounting variation.} For textured rotor $r$, $x_r$ is the log-ratio of its 1 October
mean to its August–September run, a separate mounting, so
$\operatorname{Var}(x)=2\sigma_m^2+1.5\sigma^2$. Treating the three $x_r$ as zero-mean, which
counts any common day offset as mounting variation, gives
$\sigma_m^2=(\overline{x^2}-1.5\sigma^2)/2$ with \ddofm{} degrees of freedom. A 1 October
comparison of two rotors, each mounted once, then has
$\mathrm{SE}^2=2\sigma_m^2+\sigma^2(1/n_a+1/n_b)$, with $t_{0.975,\ddofm}$ (the Satterthwaite
approximation gives 2.8 degrees of freedom, which changes no conclusion). The no-texture rotor has
no August–September run; its mounting variation is assumed equal to the textured rotors'.

\textbf{Thévenin fit.} At each set point, $V=V_\mathrm{oc}-IR_\mathrm{int}$ by least squares over
all tracking dwells. Each rotor's curve is the geometric mean of its two runs. The interval on
the mean change is a $t$ interval across set points.

\textbf{Surface parameters.} The Keyence export is a height map in millimetres. Each image column
is one spanwise profile. Columns with more than 10\% missing points are dropped, short interior
gaps are filled linearly, a least-squares line is removed, and profiles are cut to the length of
the shortest one and re-centred. Pa is the mean absolute deviation of that profile. For Ra, the profile is filtered with the ISO 16610-21 Gaussian
($\alpha=\sqrt{\ln2/\pi}$), $\lambda_c=\SI{0.25}{mm}$, and $\lambda_c/2$ is discarded at each end.
The layer period is the peak of the mean power spectrum between 60 and \SI{320}{\micro m}. Values
are means over about 1000 profiles per field. For periodic profiles ISO 4288 selects
$\lambda_c=\SI{0.25}{mm}$ for spacings of 0.04–\SI{0.13}{mm}, which fits the
\SI{0.1}{mm} period of \Ra{20} and \Ra{40}. The \SI{0.2}{mm} period of the no-texture set would
call for \SI{0.8}{mm}, and the non-periodic \Ra{80} surface for \SI{2.5}{mm}, both too long for
the \sfieldy\,mm field. Ra here therefore describes texture finer than \SI{0.25}{mm} for every
set, and Pa everything up to the field length.

\section{Reprocessing of the 27 July export}\label{app:jeong}
The file \texttt{0727windturbine.csv} holds 127\,208 rows at \SI{\jlfs}{Hz}. The channel map below
was inferred from the data and should be confirmed against the lab's wiring.
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{ch1:} DC bus voltage through a 4:1 divider.
  \item \textbf{ch2:} Hall-effect current sensor, \SI{2}{A/V} about a mid-rail zero.
  \item \textbf{ch3:} once-per-revolution rotor pulse. It tracks cleanly up to 1900 rpm and misses
        about one revolution in nine at 2000 rpm.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Rebuilding \texttt{Summary\_Table.csv}.} With $L=\lfloor127208/17\rfloor=7482$, window
$k=0\dots15$ covers samples $kL+1870$ to $kL+5611$: the middle half of each of the first 16 of 17
equal slices. With $V=4\,\mathrm{ch1}$ and $I=2(\mathrm{ch2}-2.5)$, \texttt{Vdc\_max},
\texttt{Idc\_max} and \texttt{Pdc\_max} are the separate maxima of $V$, $I$ and $VI$ over the
window. \texttt{Measured\_RPM} is 60 times the number of rising ch3 edges over the window duration.
All 64 tabulated values reproduce to floating-point precision.

\textbf{What the rebuild shows.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item The current channel carries about \SI{\jlnoise}{mA} of white noise, load on or off. At
        each window's \texttt{Pdc\_max} sample the current is \jlspikelo–\SI{\jlspikehi}{A} above
        its \SI{1}{s} mean. Applied to a stretch with the load disconnected, the recipe returns
        \SI{\jlnullp}{W}.
  \item The load was held in constant current, raised by hand, and off for the first three
        settings.
  \item Settings were changed by hand, with dwells of 12 to about \SI{40}{s}, so fixed windows can straddle a
        change: the 1300 rpm window contains the step to 1400 rpm.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Reprocessing.} Same windows; current zero from ch2 after the load is switched off
(\SI{\jlzero}{V}); power averaged over a centred \SI{1}{s} window before taking the maximum.
