% --------------------------------------------------------------- abstract --
\section*{Abstract}

Fuzzy skin is a 3D-printing setting that roughens a part's outer surface by displacing its outer
wall at random, up to a set thickness~\cite{prusa}. Roughness is known to change the performance
of airfoils~\cite{wilcox} and cylinders~\cite{achenbach}, but not of the thin curved-plate blades
of the small vertical-axis rotor studied here. We fitted this rotor in turn with four printed blade
sets: one without fuzzy skin (Plain) and three with fuzzy-skin thicknesses of
\SIlist{0.050;0.101;0.202}{mm} (FS~0.05, FS~0.10, FS~0.20). Each rotor was swept twice through
\nSP{} wind speeds from \vLo{} to \SI{\vHi}{m/s} in the URI wind tunnel on 1 October 2026, with an
electronic load measuring peak electrical power. Averaged over wind speed, FS~0.10 and FS~0.20
produced $\cPB\%$ $[\cPBlo,\ \cPBhi]$ and $\cPC\%$ $[\cPClo,\ \cPChi]$ more peak power than Plain
(Tukey simultaneous 95\% intervals). The FS~0.05 difference, $\cPA\%$ $[\cPAlo,\ \cPAhi]$, was not
resolved. The gains were largest at \bandLo--\SI{\bandHi}{m/s}. At light load FS~0.10 and FS~0.20
also turned $\spdPB\%$ and $\spdPC\%$ faster than Plain. Rotor speed measured at every load step
gives the same generator constants for every rotor (\tkKLo--\SI{\tkKHi}{mV/rpm},
\tkRLo--\SI{\tkRHi}{\ohm}), so the gain arises in the rotor and its mounting, not in the
generator. Measured roughness (Ra, Pa) did not rank the sets by power; more surface measurement is
needed to test whether it does. Each setting was one print, mounted once and run in order of
increasing thickness, and Plain also had coarser layers (\SI{0.2}{mm} against \SI{0.1}{mm}). The
gains therefore cannot yet be attributed to fuzzy skin alone; replicate prints, remounting and an
interleaved run order would settle this. Until then, rotors compared on this rig, including in the
planned fault tests, should be printed with one recorded set of print settings.

% ================================================================ 1 INTRO ==
\section{Introduction}\label{sec:scope}

Printed blades carry surface texture even without fuzzy skin: the Plain set tested here has
\SI{\pitchP}{mm} layer lines. If texture changes rotor output, print settings must be controlled
whenever rotors are compared, including in the fault tests planned for this rig
(\secref{sec:faults}), and texture becomes a design variable. Fuzzy skin makes the texture
adjustable without changing the CAD model: the slicer moves points of the outer wall in or out by a
random distance up to the set thickness~\cite{prusa}.

Roughness lowers the maximum lift and lift-to-drag ratio of airfoil sections~\cite{wilcox} and
moves the drop in a circular cylinder's drag to a lower Reynolds number~\cite{achenbach}. The blades
tested here are thin curved plates with square edges, at chord Reynolds numbers of
$\reLo\times10^4$ to $\reHi\times10^5$. Neither result predicts the effect on such blades, so it
was measured. The tests address four questions:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Does peak electrical power differ between the blade sets? (\secref{sec:comparison},
        conclusion~1)
  \item Do the differences depend on wind speed? (\secref{sec:windspeed}, conclusion~2)
  \item Does a difference arise in the rotor or in the generator? (\secref{sec:source},
        conclusion~3)
  \item Does measured surface roughness rank the sets by power? (\secref{sec:roughness},
        conclusion~4)
\end{enumerate}
The report covers the 1 October 2026 session only. A fifth blade set run that day is excluded
because its run log records its texture as unknown. The report also responds to two earlier
requests: blade identity (\tabref{tab:specimens}) and an outline for a fault-testing plan
(\secref{sec:faults}).

\section{Test setup}\label{sec:setup}

\subsection{Rotor and blade sets}\label{sec:rotor}
The rotor has three straight blades attached at $R=\SI{\geoR}{m}$ from the axis, with span
$H=\SI{\geoH}{m}$ (design values). Each blade is a thin curved plate, not an airfoil: it turns
through \SI{\geoArc}{\degree}, with \SI{\geoChord}{mm} chord, \SI{\geoWall}{mm} wall and square
edges. The swept area $A=2RH=\SI{\areaSw}{m^2}$ is nominal and is used only to normalise power.
Each rotor is held by a mount we made: the rotor is inserted into the mount's cylindrical shaft.
The same mount, generator and bearings were used for every rotor.

\tabref{tab:specimens} lists the four blade sets. A set mounted on the rig is called a rotor and
named after the set. The College of Engineering's I$^2$(s) Print Lab printed all sets from one CAD
model. Its Bambu Studio project specifies PLA on a \slPrinter{} with a \SI{\slNozzle}{mm} nozzle,
\SI{\slLayer}{mm} layers and \slWalls{} wall loops; fuzzy skin is painted on both faces of each
blade at a \SI{\slPoint}{mm} point distance, and only its thickness changes between sets. Plain has
no slicer record; its measured layer period indicates \SI{0.2}{mm} layers.

\subsection{Wind tunnel and measurements}\label{sec:wind}
The tunnel fan runs on an ABB ACS550 drive, commanded in rpm over Modbus (\figref{fig:rig}). Wind
speed $v$ is calculated from the fan speed $N$ with the tunnel's calibration (\appref{app:cal}):
\[ v = \calA\,N - \calB \qquad (\si{m/s};\ N \text{ in rpm}). \]
The fan-motor current at each set point agreed across all runs within \SI{\fanRange}{A}, so the fan
operating point did not change. Air temperature and pressure were not recorded, and no blockage
correction is applied.

The generator's three-phase output is rectified and drawn by a Chroma 63004-150-60 electronic load
in constant-current mode. A Python host sets the current and records the terminal voltage $V$ and
current $I$ at the end of each \SI{\dwellS}{s} step; power is $P=VI$. All rotors were measured with
the same instrument and settings.

\textbf{Rotor speed.}\label{sec:rpm} The rig's own speed input recorded nothing on 1 October.
T.~Kang recorded rotor speed with a proximity sensor triggered by a small magnet glued to one blade,
re-glued for each set, giving one pulse per revolution, sampled at \SI{\tachoFs}{Hz}. The magnet
adds an unbalanced mass to that blade; its mass and position were not recorded. His script
(\texttt{RPM.m}) reports for each set point the most frequent speed over single revolutions,
$n_0$, resolved to \resMin--\resHi\%. The load is at \SI{0}{A} while the fan settles, so we take
$n_0$ as the light-load speed; the voltage at the first load step, $V_1$, follows it closely
($V_1/n_0=\SI{\keMean}{mV/rpm}$, coefficient of variation \keCV\% over \nSpdPoints{} values). At
\spLo~rpm the load is already at \SI{\loadArmmA}{mA} while the fan settles, so that set point is not
used for $n_0$.

His per-sample records give speed at every load step. They are aligned to the rig's steps using his
generator-voltage channel alone, without any model: the two clocks differ by
\tkOffLo--\SI{\tkOffHi}{s}, with no measurable drift. Speed is taken over the last \SI{0.6}{s} of
each step from the pulse count. Of \tkRepairsAll{} tachometer faults, \tkRepairs{} missed pulses
were repaired and \tkFlagged{} steps excluded where a run of missed pulses halved the apparent
speed; \tkRepairsPA{} of the faults were on Plain and FS~0.05, at the highest speeds.

\subsection{Test procedure}\label{sec:procedure}
Each run visits \nSP{} fan set points, \spLo--\spHi~rpm in 100~rpm steps. At each set point the
host waits for the fan speed and the generator voltage to settle. The load then raises its current
in small steps (\SI{\stepmA}{mA} at 1800~rpm, scaled with $v^2$ below), holding each for
\SI{\dwellS}{s}, until power has fallen below \stopPct\% of its maximum on \stopConfirm{} steps in a
row. Each ladder of \dwellLo--\dwellHi{} steps traces power against current through its peak
(\figref{fig:ladders}). A run takes about \runMin{} minutes.

  hollow: run~2). Stars mark $\pmax$, the largest step power.}

\end{figure}

Each rotor was mounted once and run twice without removal, in the order Plain, FS~0.05, FS~0.10,
FS~0.20, from \tFirst{} to \tLast{} (\tabref{tab:session}). \nClean{} of the \nSweeps{} ladders

within \breakDiff\%. The rotor wobbled visibly on the mount at higher wind speeds. The wobble was
not measured, and whether it differed between rotors was not recorded.

% ============================================================= 3 ANALYSIS ==
\section{Analysis}\label{sec:stats}

$\pmax$ is the largest step power in a ladder. Rotors are compared as ratios: for each run, the
mean over the \nSP{} set points of $\ln(\pmax/P_\mathrm{ref})$, where $P_\mathrm{ref}$ is Plain's
geometric mean at that set point. The eight run means are compared with one-way analysis of
variance and Tukey's procedure for all six pairs~\cite{montgomery}. A difference is
\emph{resolved} when its simultaneous 95\% interval excludes zero. Whether the differences change
with wind speed is tested by the rotor $\times$ set point interaction against the scatter between
repeated runs. Details are in \appref{app:stats}.

To locate the source of a power difference, a straight line $V=V_\mathrm{oc}-IR_\mathrm{int}$ is
fitted to each ladder. $V_\mathrm{oc}$ is the extrapolated open-circuit voltage; $R_\mathrm{int}$ is
an apparent source resistance, which includes the winding, rectifier and wiring and also the rotor
slowing as the load rises. Both, and the light-load speed $n_0$, are compared between rotors as
$\pmax$ is. The light-load tip-speed ratio is $\lambda_0=2\pi n_0R/(60\,v)$, and the electrical
power coefficient is $C_{P,\mathrm{el}}=\pmax/(\tfrac12\rho A v^3)$ with standard air
($\rho=\SI{\rhoStd}{kg/m^3}$); it includes generator and rectifier losses.

With speed $n$ at every step, $V=kn-RI+b$ is fitted to each run: $k$ is the generator constant and
$R$ its electrical resistance, without the rotor slowing that $R_\mathrm{int}$ includes. The
generator torque is $k_TI$, with $k_T=60k/(2\pi)$; it excludes bearing friction and the torque that
decelerates the rotor during a step. The tip-speed ratio at each step is $\lambda=2\pi nR/(60\,v)$.

\SI{\vHi}{m/s}, and $C_{P,\mathrm{el}}$ from \cpLo\% to \cpHi\% (\figref{fig:power}).

\subsection{Dependence on wind speed}\label{sec:windspeed}
The gains change with wind speed by more than the scatter between repeated runs
($F_{\dfIntA,\dfIntB}=\Fint$, $p\pInt$; \figref{fig:gain}). They are largest at
\bandLo--\SI{\bandHi}{m/s} (chord Reynolds number $\reBandLo$--$\reBandHi\times10^4$), where FS~0.20
peaks at $\gMaxC\%$ and FS~0.10 at $\gMaxB\%$. The power of FS~0.10 and FS~0.20 also rises most
steeply at a lower wind speed (\stepVB{} and \SI{\stepVC}{m/s}) than Plain's (\stepVP\,\si{m/s}).

\subsection{Rotor or generator}\label{sec:source}
Averaged over wind speed, $V_\mathrm{oc}$ of FS~0.10 and FS~0.20 differs from Plain's by $\vocB\%$
and $\vocC\%$, while $R_\mathrm{int}$ shows no resolved difference (\tabref{tab:rotor}). Because the
peak power of the fitted source scales with $V_\mathrm{oc}^2$, the higher $V_\mathrm{oc}$ alone
accounts for the gains ($\vocSqB\%$ and $\vocSqC\%$ against $\cPB\%$ and $\cPC\%$ measured). Where
the gains are largest, a lower $R_\mathrm{int}$ supplies up to \rintShareMax\% of the gain.

At light load, FS~0.10 and FS~0.20 turn $\spdPB\%$ $[\spdPBlo,\ \spdPBhi]$ and $\spdPC\%$
$[\spdPClo,\ \spdPChi]$ faster than Plain (\figref{fig:speed}). Each speed change matches the change
in $V_1$ and $V_\mathrm{oc}$ within \spdVoltDiff{} percentage points, and the voltage per rpm shows
no resolved difference between rotors (largest $\keDiff\%$ $[\keDifflo,\ \keDiffhi]$). The speed
gain is larger from \SI{\spdSplitHi}{m/s}, where the band of largest power gains begins: \spdFromB\%
and \spdFromC\%, against \spdBelowB\% and \spdBelowC\% below it.

Measured at every load step, the generator is the same for every rotor: $k=\tkKLo$--\SI{\tkKHi}{mV/rpm}
and $R=\tkRLo$--\SI{\tkRHi}{\ohm} on all eight runs, with $V$ fitted within
\tkRmsLo--\SI{\tkRmsHi}{V}. Plain's apparent $R_\mathrm{int}$ (\rintPlainHi--\SI{\rintPlainLo}{\ohm})
is therefore about \SI{20}{\ohm} of electrical resistance plus the rotor slowing under load. At
\tkVmid{} and \SI{\vHi}{m/s}, FS~0.10 and FS~0.20 reach a higher $C_{P,\mathrm{el}}$ at a higher
tip-speed ratio, and at equal speed they deliver more generator torque (\figref{fig:cptsr}).
Averaged over wind speed, peak power occurs at $\lambda=\tkTsrP$ (Plain), \tkTsrA{} (FS~0.05),
\tkTsrB{} (FS~0.10) and \tkTsrC{} (FS~0.20). The generator's resistive loss barely changes the
gains: $\tkGenGainB\%$ and $\tkGenGainC\%$ at the generator input, against $\cPB\%$ and $\cPC\%$ at
the load.

\FloatBarrier
\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}

\textbf{Where the gain arises.} The generator behaves identically for every rotor along every load
ladder (\secref{sec:source}), so the extra power reaches its shaft: the textured rotors turn faster
and, at equal speed, deliver more torque. The data do not show whether this comes from more aerodynamic torque or less
resisting torque. All rotors used the same mount, generator and bearings, so less resisting torque
would have to come from the blade sets themselves (mass, balance or mounting, none measured) or from
a change in the shared parts during the session. Two mechanical effects are candidates: the
tachometer magnet, re-glued for each set, unbalances one blade, and the rotor wobbled at higher wind
speeds. Either could
change the resisting torque and differ between mountings.

\textbf{Why the gains depend on wind speed.} The gains peak at chord Reynolds numbers of
$\reBandLo$--$\reBandHi\times10^4$, and the textured rotors' power rises most steeply at a lower wind
speed than Plain's. A flow transition that texture moves to a lower Reynolds number could produce
this pattern, as roughness does on a circular cylinder~\cite{achenbach}. The flow was not examined,
so this remains a hypothesis.

\textbf{Roughness does not explain the ranking yet.} Ra and Pa from one field per set may not
represent the blades, may not capture the surface property that matters for this flow, or the power
differences may not come from texture at all. More surface measurement is needed to test whether
roughness ranks power (Recommendation~5).

% ================================================================ 6 LIMITS ==
\section{Limitations}\label{sec:limits}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{One print, one mounting.} Each texture level is one print mounted once, so print and
        mounting differences are confounded with texture. Plain also has coarser layers
        (\SI{0.2}{mm}); the three fuzzy-skin sets share \SI{0.1}{mm} layers, so their comparisons
        (FS~0.10 and FS~0.20 over FS~0.05: $\cAB\%$ and $\cAC\%$) are not affected by layer height.
  \item \textbf{Run order.} The rotors ran in order of increasing texture. Fitting a linear drift
        over the session ($\driftRate\%$ per hour, $[\driftLo,\ \driftHi]$) leaves FS~0.10 and
        FS~0.20 resolved from Plain ($\cdB\%$ and $\cdC\%$) but could explain the FS~0.05
        difference. A step change between rotors cannot be separated from a rotor effect.
  \item \textbf{Mechanical.} The tachometer magnet on one blade unbalances each rotor, and the rotor
        wobbled on the mount at higher wind speeds. Neither was measured, so neither can be
        excluded as a cause of the differences.
  \item \textbf{Measurement.} The \SI{1}{s} load steps are too short for full equilibrium: speed is
        still falling at the end of a step (median \tkSettleMed\%, more than 2\% in \tkSettleTwo\% of
        steps), alike for all rotors, so absolute peak powers are slightly high. The generator
        constant fell \tkKDriftPct\% over the session, probably as the generator warmed; this works
        slightly against the later, textured rotors. The generator torque excludes bearing friction.
  \item \textbf{Other.} Wind speed comes from the tunnel
        calibration (\appref{app:cal}), which affects absolute wind speed and $C_{P,\mathrm{el}}$
        but not the comparison between rotors, made at identical fan speeds. Air density was not
        recorded, and no blockage correction is applied.
\end{itemize}

% =========================================================== 7 CONCLUSIONS ==
\section{Conclusions}\label{sec:conclusions}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item FS~0.10 and FS~0.20 produced $\cPB\%$ $[\cPBlo,\ \cPBhi]$ and $\cPC\%$ $[\cPClo,\ \cPChi]$
        more peak electrical power than Plain, averaged over \vLo--\vHi\,\si{m/s}. The FS~0.05
        difference, $\cPA\%$ $[\cPAlo,\ \cPAhi]$, was not resolved.
  \item The gains were largest at \bandLo--\SI{\bandHi}{m/s}, where FS~0.20 reached $\gMaxC\%$.
  \item The gains came with faster rotation ($\spdPB\%$ and $\spdPC\%$ at light load) and more
        torque at equal speed, while the generator's constants, measured at every load step, were the
        same for every rotor. They arise in the rotor and its mounting, not in the generator.
  \item Ra and Pa from one field per set did not rank the rotors by power; more surface measurement
        is needed to test whether roughness does.
  \item With one print and one mounting per texture, a fixed run order, coarser layers on Plain and
        unmeasured mechanical effects (magnet imbalance, wobble), the gains cannot yet be attributed
        to fuzzy skin alone.
\end{enumerate}

% ================================================================= 8 NEXT ==
\section{Recommendations}\label{sec:next}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Replicate and randomise.} Print at least two sets per thickness, Plain included,
        from one saved project with \SI{0.1}{mm} layers. Weigh and label every blade. Mount each
        set twice, run in an interleaved order, and re-run Plain after every third rotor.
  \item \textbf{Stiffen and balance the mount.} Extend the cylindrical shaft that the rotor is
        inserted into, to reduce the wobble at higher wind speeds. Balance the tachometer magnet
        with equal masses on the other two blades, or move it to the shaft. On the redesigned base
        plate, connect the rig's tunnel node (an Arduino Nano 33 BLE Sense), which logs air
        temperature, pressure and vibration, on every run.
  \item \textbf{Log rotor speed at every step on the rig clock,} on the rig's own pulse input; this
        revision aligned T.~Kang's records after the fact. Use longer load steps, since the
        \SI{1}{s} steps did not settle. A spin-down test of each mounted rotor would separate
        aerodynamic from resisting torque.
  \item \textbf{Extend the wind calibration} over the full test range, and record air temperature
        and pressure on every run.
  \item \textbf{Measure the surfaces more fully:} several fields per set, at fixed locations on two
        blades and both faces, with evaluation lengths of at least five cut-offs. This is needed to
        test whether roughness ranks the rotors by power.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Toward the fault-testing plan}\label{sec:faults}
Prof.\ Jeong asked for a fault-testing plan agreed before the next round. The rig gives an
automated sweep of about \runMin{} minutes, with a run-to-run repeatability of \sigRun\% in the run
mean and \sigE\% at a single set point. Blade sets printed with different settings differed by up to
$\cPC\%$, so fault blades and their reference set should be printed from one saved project, with
the reference re-run between fault cases. The reference rotor should be balanced first, because the tachometer magnet already unbalances
one blade. Candidate faults are a tip mass on one blade (imbalance),
a chipped, shortened or missing blade, added bearing drag, and an open or high-resistance generator
phase. Whether $V_\mathrm{oc}$ and $R_\mathrm{int}$ can tell rotor faults from electrical ones is
untested; the first cases should test this with rotor speed logged at every step. We propose a short
meeting to agree the fault list, severity levels and acceptance criteria before any fault blades
are printed.

\section{Statistical details}\label{app:stats}
\textbf{Model.} With $y_{rjs}=\ln\pmax$ for rotor $r$ (4), run $j$ (2) and set point $s$ (14),
\[ y_{rjs} = \mu + \alpha_r + \beta_s + (\alpha\beta)_{rs} + u_{rj} + e_{rjs}, \]

rotor effect is tested against run within rotor (the one-way ANOVA on run means), and the

variance across set points, so it is approximate; it was repeated without the two set points with

\textbf{Comparisons.} With pooled run-to-run standard deviation $\sigma=\sigRun\%$ (\dofRun{}
degrees of freedom), Tukey's interval for rotors $a$ and $b$ is
$\exp\!\big(\bar y_b-\bar y_a \pm q_{0.05;4,4}\,\sigma/\sqrt2\big)-1$ with $q_{0.05;4,4}=\qTukey$, a
half-width of about \hwTukey\%. An exact permutation test of the rotor effect over all \permN{}
splits of the run means gives $p=\permP$. The parabolic-fit estimator changes the differences by at
most \fitMaxDiff{} percentage points.

\textbf{Mounting variation.} If each rotor's mean carried an independent mounting offset with
standard deviation $\tau$, a difference would stay resolved while
$|\bar y_b-\bar y_a|>(q_{0.05;4,4}/\sqrt2)\sqrt{\sigma^2+2\tau^2}$.

\textbf{Drift.} The run means are fitted as $\bar y_{rj}=a_r+\beta t_{rj}$, with $t_{rj}$ the run
start time in hours. The slope rests mostly (\driftShare\%) on the two Plain runs, the only pair an
hour apart.

\section{Surface analysis}\label{app:surface}
Each image column of the Keyence height map is one profile across the layer lines. Columns with too
many missing points are dropped (\rejLo\% or fewer for Plain, FS~0.05 and FS~0.10; \rejC\% for
FS~0.20, whose values may therefore be understated). Each profile is levelled; Pa is its mean
absolute deviation. Ra uses the Gaussian mean line of ISO~16610-21~\cite{iso16610} with
$\lambda_c=\SI{0.25}{mm}$ over a \evalLo--\SI{\evalHi}{mm} evaluation length. The layer period is the
peak of the mean spectrum between 60 and \SI{320}{\micro m}. ISO~4288~\cite{iso4288} recommends
this cut-off for the \SI{0.1}{mm} layer lines of FS~0.05 and FS~0.10, but longer ones for Plain's
\SI{0.2}{mm} lines and for FS~0.20; both exceed the \fieldY\,mm field. Ra therefore keeps about
\transP\% of Plain's layer-line amplitude and \transA\% of the \SI{0.1}{mm} ones.
