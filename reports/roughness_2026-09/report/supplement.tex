\vspace{0.4em}

T.~Kang's tachometer records (360 samples per second) for the eight runs were shared on
6 October. They are aligned to the rig's 1~s load steps using his generator-voltage channel alone:
the two clocks differ by \tkOffLo--\SI{\tkOffHi}{s}, with no measurable drift. This gives rotor
speed at all \tkDwells{} load steps (\tkRepairs{} missed pulses repaired, \tkFlagged{} steps excluded).

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{The generator is the same for every rotor, at every load step.} With speed measured,
        $V=kn-RI+b$ gives $k=\tkKLo$--\SI{\tkKHi}{mV/rpm} and $R=\tkRLo$--\SI{\tkRHi}{\ohm} on all
        eight runs. The report's inference that the gain
        arises in the rotor, drawn from light-load data, now holds along every load ladder. Plain's
        apparent source resistance in the report (\rintPlainHi--\SI{\rintPlainLo}{\ohm}) is about
        \SI{20}{\ohm} of electrical resistance plus the rotor slowing under load.
  \item \textbf{Power coefficient and torque} (\figref{fig:s1}). At \tkVmid{} and \SI{\vHi}{m/s},
        FS~0.10 and FS~0.20 reach a higher $C_{P,\mathrm{el}}$ at a higher tip-speed ratio, and
        their torque--speed curves sit at higher speed. Averaged over wind speed, peak power occurs at
        $\lambda=\tkTsrP$ (Plain), \tkTsrA{} (FS~0.05), \tkTsrB{} (FS~0.10) and \tkTsrC{}
        (FS~0.20). The generator's resistive loss barely changes the gains:
        $\tkGenGainB\%$ and $\tkGenGainC\%$ at the generator input, against $\cPB\%$ and $\cPC\%$ at
        the load.
  \item \textbf{The 1~s steps are too short for full equilibrium.} Speed is still falling at the end
        of a step (median \tkSettleMed\%; more than 2\% in \tkSettleTwo\% of steps), alike for all
        rotors. Comparisons hold; absolute peak powers are slightly high.
  \item \textbf{The generator constant fell} by \SI{\tkKDrift}{mV/rpm} per hour
        $[\tkKDriftLo,\ \tkKDriftHi]$, \tkKDriftPct\% over the \SI{\tkSpanH}{h} session, probably as
        the generator warmed. The textured rotors ran later, so this works slightly against them.
\end{enumerate}

\vspace{-0.8em}
{\small\textbf{Still open.} The data do not yet separate more aerodynamic torque from less resisting
torque: a flow effect that depends on wind speed and losses that grow with speed both fit. The torque
shown is the generator's electromagnetic torque, without bearing friction or the rotor's deceleration.
The tachometer magnet was re-glued for each set; only Plain and FS~0.05 lost pulses at the highest
speeds, consistent with a different magnet gap. Absolute $C_{P,\mathrm{el}}$ and $\lambda$ carry the
wind-calibration uncertainty. \textbf{Next round:} a redesigned base plate with a longer shaft, a
balanced tachometer target, longer load steps, and an Arduino Nano 33 BLE Sense logging air
conditions and vibration.}
